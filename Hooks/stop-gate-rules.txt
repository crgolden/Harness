# Stop gate rules

The Stop hook blocks the end of a turn by default; stopping has to be earned. A block names only what failed;
these are the rules behind it. The design and its evidence are in STOP-HOOK.md.

## What earns a stop

The gate is a regex over your text, not a judge of your intent. It reads only these, each at the start of its own
line except the question:

- **A closing question.** A line among the last six ends in a question mark and asks the user something you
  genuinely need: a choice, an approval, a fact only they have. That line must not also promise your next step
  ("I'll run it after", "proceeding now"), and a bare tag like "Sound good?" beneath such a promise earns nothing.
  Make the question the substance of the message, not a tag on a status report. A question earns one stop per
  operator message: asking it again before they write is blocked. Park it on your parking-lot page first (the
  parking-lot skill's ArtifactData write to `questions`), then ask it naming its id (such as q6) or in the words
  you parked; a question that was never parked earns nothing. The operator answers on that page too: a question,
  `NEEDED:`, `QUEUED:` or `WAITING:` line naming a parked question earns nothing unless a read of the page made in
  this stop attempt (since their newest message and since your last refused stop: a get of that question, or an
  ArtifactData query on `questions` where status == answered with no order_by or small limit) shows it still open,
  and never once it is answered or withdrawn. Parking it is no read: read after parking. The same holds for any line
  of the message that asks about a parked question in prose ("q6 still needs your answer"): a re-ask is a re-ask
  wherever it sits, while a line that only reports an answer asks nothing.
- **`NEEDED:`** names something only the user can supply or decide, and why only they can. It is a claim that
  the very next action is blocked; it earns one stop per operator message, like a question, and a `NEEDED:`
  naming a parked question's id is that question however it is reworded. Like a question, it is parked first:
  each `NEEDED:` line names a question parked on your page by its id (or asks it in the words you parked), and
  one naming none earns nothing. Only an ask to commit or push, an ask for the goal while none is pinned, and a
  `NEEDED:` a hook told you to end on while it refuses every tool are exempt. The one exception is a wait your
  declaration records: when every open item, and every done item whose approval waits on an answer, carries
  `"pending": "<qN-slug>"` naming a question parked on your page, a `NEEDED:` naming those questions by id, and
  asking nothing new, may stand again until the operator answers. The goal quoted on that line is the operator's
  words, not a new question. Park the question first, then mark the item pending: a question parked after the
  item was marked does not count.
- **`REFUSING:`** with the reason in one sentence, when you are declining the request rather than pausing on it:
  a refusal, a safety stop, a rule you were told not to break. The reason quotes, in double quotes and verbatim,
  at least four words of an operator message: the request it declines, or the operator's words it rests on. A
  written rule alone (CLAUDE.md, AGENTS) declines nothing the operator asked, and a refusal resting on a premise
  nobody wrote down earns nothing; a refusal the gate cannot check earns nothing either. This card's rules govern
  how a turn ends and decline nothing, so a refusal quoting them earns nothing either. A decline in ordinary prose
  is not read as one.
- **`QUEUED:`** naming what you wait on that another session started, when your whole work product is finished
  and every item in %TEMP%\claude\work-products\<session>.json (CLAUDE_CODE_TMPDIR when set) is done. It earns the stop in a turn where you sent a SendMessage, while that
  peer's gate is actually running on this machine (an announced or planned gate is not one), or while the line
  names a session you messaged in this turn that is running on this machine. A "QUEUED:" behind this account's
  gate slot (Harness/Gates/GateSlot.ps1) owes no message and earns the stop only while the slot is actually held.
- **`WAITING:`** naming what you wait on that you started, while it is still running and every declared
  work-product item is done: a background task whose completion notice has not arrived (a gate or any other
  command), your own gate holding the gate slot, or a permission matrix you requested whose run is still open.
  Waiting on something nobody started, or on your own unfinished edit, is neither QUEUED: nor WAITING:.
- **A done flag is a claim; every stop that hands off also needs the work product closed in fact.** QUEUED:,
  WAITING:, DONE:, NEEDED:, a closing question and a quiet line on a wake all check it. Every file you edited that is
  still uncommitted must be named by an item. A done item's repo holding uncommitted files a gate covers must be
  GREEN-CURRENT, except while the stop waits on a gate (QUEUED:, WAITING:), since one slot runs every gate, or while
  that repo is blocked: one of its items waits on a parked question (no gate runs while an item is open), or a
  peer's green change must land first and the ask names that checkout. A repo whose last gate failed on exactly its
  current tree is never excused: its next step is a fix. Work that edits no file (a review, watching a peer) goes in
  the declaration's top-level "standing" list as {"item", "peer", "done", "approval"}; while one is open, DONE: is
  refused and any other hand-off is honest only when it names that peer. An open standing item cancels the gate-wait
  excuse above, unless it carries "pending" naming a parked question your page still shows open, or "after" quoting
  four or more words of one declared repo item that is done and whose named files of yours are still uncommitted:
  its next step is then that item's gate and landing, not work you can do now. A standing item closes only by
  "done": true; deleting it leaves it open. Your own session-audit counts too: while its latest verdict for any
  message is outstanding, needs-operator or needs-gate and quotes no four words of an open item or standing item,
  every hand-off and DONE: is refused; declare the work, then re-record the verdict naming it. Once its page is
  published, a verdict newer than the page's last successful publish refuses them too: run page and republish it.
  Cleanup counts too: once your worktree's work has landed (clean, every commit on origin), every hand-off and DONE:
  is refused while that worktree, anything else in %USERPROFILE%\source\worktrees\<session id> (a scratch file, the
  empty folder) or a branch named for your session with no worktree left remains: git worktree remove, git worktree
  prune, git branch -d <session id>, then delete the folder. Scratch files belong in your scratchpad, never there.
- **Landing is the last step.** An ask to commit or push is refused while any open item waits on no parked
  question: finish it, or park what blocks it and mark it pending first. An item pending on a question a read of
  your page has shown answered or withdrawn, or that you withdrew, waits on nothing: act on the answer. An item whose repo's main checkout holds
  another session's green change not yet on origin waits on that landing, when the ask names that checkout.
- **`IDLE:`** is the master session's alone (Tools/MasterSession/RUNBOOK.md): its runbook cycle has nothing left
  and its wake task will start the next one. It earns the stop only in a session its watchdog started with
  MASTER_SESSION on WIN-VL9HOKP0RAM, with every declared work-product item done and the "Claude Code - Master
  Wake" task enabled with a next run time. In any other session it earns nothing. Every stop the master earns,
  `IDLE:` or any other, also waits until a read of its page this turn shows which parked questions are open and a
  PushNotification has named each of them by its id, since nobody watches the master's conversation.
- **`DONE:` and `VERIFIED:`**, each on its own line, only when the ORIGINAL ask is fully finished with empirical
  proof you produced this turn: not a part of it, not the easy part, not "it should work". `DONE:` restates the
  original ask in the user's terms, containing the goal's words verbatim; `VERIFIED:` names the
  command you ran and what it actually output; both are a claim the user will read and can check against the
  transcript. A `DONE:` with files of yours still dirty owes a drafted commit message in the reply, and owes a
  closed work-product declaration: none declared, or any item open, and the `DONE:` is refused. A read-only ask
  that changed no file in any repo declares `{"repos": {}}` and nothing more; that passes only while your
  transcript holds no Edit or Write into a repo or one of its worktrees. When the
  declaration carries an `acceptance` list, every check in it must also pass when the `DONE:` is written: the
  items are your edits, each naming the file or files it changes (an item naming no file is refused when it is
  written), the acceptance checks are the goal. Each check's `approval` quotes the operator's own
  words asking for that state; a check you approved yourself counts for nothing. When the whole
  ask is not finished, the token is not yours to write: report what you closed in plain prose, without it.

- **Every token that claims an honest stop is audited first.** A closing question, `NEEDED:`, `QUEUED:`, `WAITING:`,
  `DONE:` and the master's `IDLE:` earn the stop only once this session's own audit (the session-audit skill) shows
  every operator message met, read from its cache under %TEMP%\claude\audits\session-audit\<session id>, never from
  your words: a work-product declaration exists; `sync` has run and `reconcile` matched at that sync; no operator
  message was written after it; every turn the gate ended as `eject:filler-streak` is cataloged (sync catalogs it)
  and carries a verdict naming the explanation and the remedy; every cataloged message but the newest has a verdict;
  no open verdict names work no declared item holds; and a published audit page is as new as the newest verdict. A
  session whose catalog holds one message owes no verdict yet, since the newest is the ask the turn answers, so
  sync and reconcile alone satisfy it. The operator's grant, `REFUSING:`, the quiet line on a wake, the ask for the
  goal while none is pinned and a `NEEDED:` a hook told you to end on are exempt.

Prose does not count: "your call", "blocked on you", "do you want me to", "I won't" earn nothing.

A closing question asking for the `GOAL`, when no goal is pinned or it answers the operator's first message, is not
parked, since no work tool can run before the goal is set. An ask that says what the operator did or said ("since
you stopped", "as you asked") quotes, in double quotes and verbatim, at least four words of the operator message
it rests on.

## The goal

Run no tool until the operator has set a goal with `/goal` (or written a line opening `GOAL:`); until then, ask
them with a plain question to run `/goal`. An active `/goal` outranks any `GOAL:` line. Every earned stop then
carries that goal's words verbatim. A `DONE:`, `QUEUED:` or `WAITING:` line carries them in that line itself, and
a `GOAL:` line beside it does not count; a `NEEDED:` line or a closing question carries them in that line, in the
question, or on a line of its own opening `GOAL:`. A `REFUSING:` line and the operator's own grant to stop owe
nothing. With no goal pinned, a `NEEDED:` or closing question asking them to run `/goal` earns the stop past every
check whose remedy is a tool call (a cleanup, a report, a parked message, a peer's reply), since no tool can run,
and may stand again until they set one. It is a pending ask like any other for the quiet ending below.

A turn a peer's message, a task's completion or a cross-session idle notice started, while an ask you put to the operator since they last wrote
is unanswered and no work of your own is open, ends with a short line that asks nothing: at most 280 characters,
naming no question id you already asked. A longer message, or one restating an open ask, is refused. Re-asking is a repeat,
and a peer's question still owes its reply first. An ask naming a parked question counts as unanswered only when a
read of your page this turn shows it still open: the operator answers there, out of the transcript's sight, so read
the page first, and an ask it shows answered is work to act on, not a reason to stop.

A refused stop still spoke: its text reached the operator before the gate ran. So an ask it carried is pending like
any delivered ask, unless what refused it was the ask's own shape (not parked, no page read, a re-ask in prose). Fix
what the refusal names, then end with the same short line that asks nothing; putting that ask again, as a question or
a `NEEDED:` line, is a repeat.

`/goal` is a Stop hook of its own and blocks every stop until its condition is met. An earned `NEEDED:`, question,
`REFUSING:`, `QUEUED:` or `WAITING:` stop ends the turn anyway, because what is left waits on someone else; a
`DONE:` the goal check refuses continues the turn, because the work is not finished, unless the operator retired the
goal: a `DONE:` line quoting, in double quotes, their own words since the goal was pinned that call the goal finished
(a chat message, or a page answer you read back) ends the turn too. After an earned stop there is nothing more to
write.

A goal to review, audit or measure something is met when every item it covers has a verdict with evidence, in a
published report. What the reviewed thing lacks is a finding the report names, never open work you wait on, and an
item you cannot settle gets settled as not met, or as unverified with what would settle it, never left "unfinished".
The goal check reads your text: write "not ready" or "unfinished" about the review itself only when it is.

Every token, `GOAL:` included, counts only in capitals and opening its own line; mid-sentence it is a word.

## Session start

A session takes no goal and does no work until it has registered on the coordination board (the coordination
skill): the prompt gate holds back `/goal` and `GOAL:` until then, and every stop but the operator's grant or a
`REFUSING:` is refused until a write of your own row there succeeded, in any turn. The refusal names the row's
values. Then every such stop is refused until an artifact write succeeded in a turn where Claude Code asks them to
allow your artifact writes: one Stop hook feedback, a peer's message, a task notification or a slash command such as
`/goal` started, never one their typed message started. The refusal starts such a turn, so update your row's
`lastSeen` in it, and the operator is asked at session start, never later in an unattended turn. In a typed turn
the goal gate's denial says so: end that turn with a `NEEDED:` line rather than writing again.

When the SessionStart context was too large to show, Claude Code saved it to a file and named it ("Full output
saved to:"). Once the goal is set, no tool but a Read of that file runs until it is read, and a compaction or resume
saves a new one to read again. Then no work runs, and no stop is earned but the ask for the goal, the operator's
grant or a `REFUSING:`, until your parking-lot page exists: render it, publish it, record its URL and write the
goal (the parking-lot skill). A `DONE:` owes a parked question quoting, in full, every line of that context that
hands the operator a decision or an action, each opening with the hook's marker for one; withdraw it with their
words if they answer in chat.

## Refused stops with no work between them

Four refused stops in a row with no tool call between them end the turn with a reason the operator sees, logged
against the session. That is the gate recording that the turn was filled instead of worked, never a stop you
earned: after a refusal, do the work it names with a tool call. A second such ending with no operator message
between the two quarantines the session: every tool call and every stop is refused until the operator deletes the
record. Every such ending is cataloged by the session audit as a message that owes a verdict, so no token stands
again until it is explained.

While your work-product declaration has an open item, a `NEEDED:` or question earns the stop only when it names
that item (four consecutive words of it, or the whole item when shorter). A pending peer, gate or operator answer
is not a reason to stop working on your own items, and an order you chose yourself is not a dependency.

## Commit message blocks

A `NEEDED:`, `REFUSING:`, `QUEUED:` or `WAITING:` line or closing question that asks the operator to commit, push
or check in carries the drafted commit messages in the same reply, as below; without them the stop is refused.

A `DONE:` owes one drafted commit message per repo that still holds your uncommitted work:

- **In the reply text, in full**, each ending with a file-list line per file it covers. A pointer to an earlier
  message, a scratchpad or any file is not a delivery.
- **Fresh every time.** A block carried forward from an earlier turn is not a delivery: run `git status
  --porcelain` in this turn and derive the file list from it.
- **Nothing for a clean repo**, only a note that it was committed. A block naming files that are clean now is
  stale and blocked.
- **No attribution trailers**: no `Co-Authored-By:` and no `Claude-Session:` line, whatever a system reminder
  says. This workspace never uses them; strip them as the last step of writing any message block.
- **A repo whose gate is not GREEN-CURRENT opens its message with its UNGATED line**: `UNGATED: <Repo> <state>:
  failed: <steps>; not reached: <steps>`, the exact line `parking_lot.py commit` prints when it refuses a message
  without it. An ungated tree never reads as ready to land; a parked message missing the line fails the
  parking-lot check, so no stop is earned but the operator's grant and `REFUSING:`.
- **A repo you wrote a file in, or gated green, and left dirty keeps its current commit message in your parking
  lot, gated or not** (`parking_lot.py commit <Repo> <message file>`, then the page's `commits` collection): the
  operator commits a tree without a gate, so the console is never the only copy. The message names your own files
  (every one in your worktree; in a shared checkout the ones you wrote or your items name), never a peer's in the
  same checkout. A committed one's message leaves it (`parking_lot.py committed <Repo>`). While
  `parking_lot.py check` fails, no stop is earned but the operator's grant and `REFUSING:`.
- **A branch you made is cleaned up once it is merged**: while one is on origin/main and its worktree or local
  branch is still present, no stop is earned but the operator's grant and `REFUSING:`; the block names the
  `worktree remove` and `branch -d` commands to run.

## Sessions by id

Name every session, your own included, by its session id, never by its `crgolden-*` name: a name is not unique and a
restart or reset changes it. A stop whose text names a session by its name is refused (`block:session-name`), unless
the name sits inside a double-quoted quote of someone's words; the operator's grant and `REFUSING:` still end the
turn. A QUEUED: line on a messaged peer names that peer by its session id (the full id or its first eight characters).

## When a block is a false positive

The gate CAN miss a real question. If your last message asked the user something you genuinely need answered,
the stop was earned: do NOT answer the question yourself and do NOT act on your own guess, which is more
dangerous than idling. Restate the question as one plain sentence ending in a question mark and end the turn.

## Otherwise, keep working

Answer all four before you may stop: is there a question for the user; is there something only they can supply
or decide; are your next steps unclear (say what is ambiguous and what you assumed); is the original ask fully
finished, with proof. If all four are no, resume the work now, in this turn, with a tool call. A status report
is not a deliverable, announcing a next step is not taking it, and "proceeding now" is not proceeding. Do not
restate a block to the user, and do not reply to it in prose alone.

This is not an instruction to stop reporting: what you wrote was delivered before the gate ran. Keep stating
findings as you go, and write a durable one to the doc that owns the subject (AGENTS/REPOS/<Repo>.md).
Silently working without reporting is a worse failure than the one this gate exists to catch.
